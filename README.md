# SSOM RFCs

The **Standardized Semantic Object Model (SSOM)** is a draft, vendor-neutral, evidence-aware semantic standard for industrial operations data. SSOM models industrial **Assets** as the canonical lifecycle entities for identity, identifier continuity, relationships, condition, measurement safety, and a governed operational truth-state lifecycle from observation through verified work outcome.

SSOM standardizes **meaning**, not transport, control behavior, workflow execution engines, dashboard behavior, cloud deployment, or vendor-specific product implementation. SSOM reuses the generic Recommendation, Decision, Action, and Outcome foundation for maintenance and reliability semantics, and now adds first-class schemas for failure, work verification, measurable work outcome, event, alarm, and state-transition semantics.

## Why SSOM exists

Operational data is generated by many systems, facilities, equipment types, platforms, and vendors. It is often difficult to use across those boundaries because it lacks:

- Shared semantic meaning
- Portable asset identity
- Time-aware identifier continuity and succession
- Explicit relationships and topology
- Provenance and source authority
- Quality and temporal context
- Safe quantity, unit, calibration, and timing semantics for measurement comparison
- A consistent, machine-readable representation suitable for analytics and AI

SSOM addresses that gap by defining a common semantic model for operational records, evidence, and governed truth states.

SSOM does not treat every operational record as a fact. Observations, source assertions, derived assertions, inferences, predictions, recommendations, decisions, actions, and outcomes remain distinguishable so implementations can preserve evidence, conflict, correction, and supersession.

SSOM also distinguishes original source measurement from canonical normalized measurement so quantity kind, unit meaning, conversion lineage, quality state, calibration state, and timing context remain explicit.

Practical warning:

> A value is not operationally comparable merely because it is numeric.

## Assets, Classes, and Boundaries

SSOM uses **Asset** as the canonical lifecycle identity for any physical or logical entity that participates in industrial operations. **Equipment** and **Device** are distinct operational classifications of an Asset. They are **not** mutually exclusive top-level types. SSOM also distinguishes an Asset instance from an Asset class, an equipment model, a functional location, and non-serialized operational boundaries such as systems, process segments, production units, and control entities.

```text
Asset Instance
  ├── asset_class_ref -> Asset Class
  ├── equipment_model_ref -> Equipment Model
  ├── asset_form -> Component | Assembly | Instrument | Controller | Logical Asset | ...
  ├── roles -> Equipment and/or Device
  ├── installed_in -> Functional Location
  ├── related_to -> System | Subsystem | Functional System | Process Segment | Production Unit | Control Entity
  └── lifecycle identity, observations, actions, outcomes, and conditions
```

Practical examples:

- Pump `P-101` is normally an Asset with `asset_form = machine` and `equipment_roles = [process_equipment]`.
- PLC `PLC-17` is normally an Asset with `asset_form = controller` and `device_roles = [control_device, compute_device]`.
- VFD `VFD-12` is an Asset that can legitimately be both `equipment_roles = [utility_equipment]` and `device_roles = [control_device, actuation_device]`.

SSOM does not force Equipment and Device into mutually exclusive inheritance branches. An Asset may be classified as Equipment, Device, both, or neither when the available evidence does not support a stronger claim.

## Identity and Continuity

SSOM distinguishes:

- **Asset identity:** the canonical SSOM Asset identity, which is stable and never reused.
- **Asset identifier:** an external or business-specific identifier assigned to an Asset within a scope and authority.
- **Asset class:** a semantic or source classification such as pump, PLC, or chiller.
- **Equipment model:** a manufacturer or design model designation such as a vendor model code.
- **Functional location:** a distinct operational or engineering location context, not the same thing as Asset identity.
- **Operational boundary:** a non-serialized organizing context such as a System, Subsystem, Functional System, Process Segment, Production Unit, or Control Entity.
- **External source reference:** a source-specific pointer retained for provenance and reconciliation.
- **Asset succession:** a governed continuity relationship such as replacement, predecessor or successor, split, merge, decommissioned-as, or recommissioned-as.

SSOM allows one Asset to have many identifiers at the same time, and it allows the same identifier value to refer to different Assets over time when validity periods do not overlap within the same scope and authority. Engineering tags, historian paths, CMDB identifiers, and OPC UA node IDs are therefore preserved as time-bound assignments, not assumed to be globally immutable identities.

## What SSOM standardizes

- Assets, asset forms, and classification assertions
- Asset classes, equipment models, and non-serialized operational boundary objects
- Canonical Asset identity, identifier assignments, scopes, authorities, aliases, and succession semantics
- Governed relationships and topology with machine-readable inverse and domain rules
- Observations and telemetry
- Quantity kind, unit reference, conversion lineage, calibration context, and signal semantics for measurement safety
- Symptom, failure mode, failure mechanism, failure cause, failure event, diagnostic, and prognostic semantics
- Source assertions and derived assertions
- Maintenance strategy, work request, work plan, work execution, work verification, and work outcome semantics
- Event, alarm, and state-transition semantics with source-state preservation and governed lifecycle transitions
- Inference and prediction records
- Recommendations, decisions, generic actions, and outcomes
- Conditions
- Functional-location references and identity lifecycle events
- Operational context
- Provenance and lineage
- Quality and confidence
- Temporal integrity
- Policy evidence
- Extensions, profiles, compatibility, and conformance

SSOM can now distinguish whether work was requested, planned, executed, verified, and measurably effective. A completed work record is not treated as proof of restored function.

## What SSOM does not standardize

SSOM does not replace or define:

- MQTT, Sparkplug, OPC UA, REST, Kafka, AMQP, Modbus, BACnet, or other transports or protocols
- SCADA, BMS, MES, historians, brokers, Industrial DataOps products, OEM systems, or control systems
- PLC logic or direct process-control commands
- Workflow engines, task queues, assignments, or case-management products
- User accounts, tenant administration, billing, credentials, support, or product configuration
- Dashboard layouts, widget models, UI state, or visualization frameworks
- A mandatory database, cloud provider, event bus, or serialization implementation
- Any requirement to share data with a third party
